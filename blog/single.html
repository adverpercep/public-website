CTV Ad Spend Forecast: 2026 Update

EH
Eric Haggstrom SVP, Forecasting & Research
April 2, 2026 · 5 min read
Share in 𝕏
Featured Image — CTV & Streaming Research 2026

Source: Advertiser Perceptions CTV Buyer Sentiment Survey, Q1 2026. Survey of 350 US media buyers.

Connected TV ad spend is on track to reach $42.4 billion in the US in 2026, according to our latest forecast — a 22% increase over 2025. But the headline number obscures important nuance about which segments of the market are growing, which are decelerating, and where advertiser confidence is lagging the investment reality.

This update draws on a Q1 2026 survey of 350 US media buyers across agency and brand sides, combined with sell-side data shared by platform partners. Here are the three most important things to know.

1. Sports streaming is the primary growth driver

Live sports rights have shifted decisively toward streaming platforms. In our survey, 68% of buyers said they had increased their streaming sports investment in the past 12 months, with 41% describing the increase as "significant." The NFL, NBA, and college sports portfolios on major streaming platforms are commanding premium CPMs that rivals in the entertainment space cannot match.

"If you're not buying streaming sports, you're not buying premium video — it's that simple now." — Agency buyer, national entertainment advertiser

Amazon, Netflix, and Apple TV+ have collectively captured a disproportionate share of sports spend growth. Legacy broadcasters with streaming arms are competing, but buyers report that attribution and measurement capabilities are more mature on the pure-play streamers.

2. FAST channel growth is accelerating — but measurement is holding spend back

Free Ad-Supported Streaming TV reached 220 million monthly active viewers in the US in Q4 2025, according to our estimates. Advertiser interest is high: 74% of buyers we surveyed are now running FAST campaigns, up from 56% in 2024.

But spend per buyer is not keeping pace with reach growth. The primary constraint is measurement: 62% of FAST buyers cite "inadequate third-party verification" as their top concern, followed by audience deduplication across platforms (54%) and brand safety context controls (48%).

The measurement gap

The sell side has work to do. Buyers who are scaling FAST investment tell us the platforms that will win long-term are those that can provide:

  • Real-time frequency capping across FAST and SVOD inventory within the same household
  • IAB Tech Lab-compliant viewability metrics (not panel-based proxies)
  • Outcome-based reporting tied to business KPIs, not just impression delivery

Several platforms are moving aggressively on this front. We expect meaningful measurement progress by H2 2026, which should unlock incremental budget from buyers currently sitting on the sidelines.

3. Bundled deals are reshaping upfront commitments

The traditional separation between linear and digital upfront commitments is eroding. In 2026, we're seeing a significant increase in "streaming-and-linear" bundled deals where buyers commit across both environments in exchange for guaranteed reach, data targeting, and price certainty.

For sellers with both linear and streaming inventory, this is a significant opportunity — but it requires more sophisticated packaging and pricing models than most have deployed historically. Buyers tell us they want flexibility: the ability to shift allocation between linear and streaming mid-campaign based on performance data.

The sellers who will win the most upfront dollars in 2026 are those who can offer this flexibility while still delivering the reach guarantees that justify upfront pricing.
